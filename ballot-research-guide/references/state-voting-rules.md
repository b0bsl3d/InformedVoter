# Verifying state and local voting rules

Voting rules vary by state, and sometimes by county or city. Verify each item below for **this election**, then record it in `voting_info` in `ballot.json` with its source.

Never fill these from memory. Rules change between elections through new laws, court rulings and emergency orders.

## Sources, in priority order

1. **State chief election office.** Usually the Secretary of State, but sometimes a state board of elections or a lieutenant governor. Find it through vote.gov or NASS's canivote.org.
2. **The county (or city/town) election office** that issued the ballot. It sets locations and hours, and sometimes early-voting days. In New England and in parts of Michigan and Wisconsin, elections are run by cities or towns.
3. **Nonpartisan aggregators**, used only to cross-check: vote.gov, canivote.org, VOTE411.org, NCSL voter-ID and early-voting tables, Ballotpedia.
4. **Local news "how to vote" explainers**, used only for recent changes. Always confirm them against 1 or 2.

## Checklist: verify each item

**Registration**
- Deadline, and whether it has already passed for this election.
- Same-day or Election Day registration: allowed or not, and what documents it needs.
- How to check registration status (link to the state lookup).
- First-time voters who registered by mail: some states (federal HAVA rule) require ID or a document at the first in-person vote if it wasn't verified at registration.

**Voter ID**
- Required: strict photo, non-strict photo, non-photo ID, or none.
- Accepted documents, in a short list. Name any notable exclusions, such as student IDs in some states.
- What happens without ID: a provisional ballot, an affidavit, and any cure deadline.

**When and where**
- Early in-person voting: whether it's offered, the first and last day, and typical hours. Note when it ends on a weekday.
- Election Day hours. Being in line at closing time generally lets you vote; confirm the state's rule.
- Vote centers (any location in the county) vs. an assigned polling place, with the locator link.
- All-mail states (e.g., CO, OR, WA, UT, NV, CA, HI, VT): the ballot arrives automatically. Give return options, drop-box hours, the postmark vs. received-by deadline, and in-person options.
- Absentee or mail voting elsewhere: excuse required or not, request deadline, return deadline, witness or notary requirements, and signature cure process.

**At the ballot**
- Straight-ticket (one-punch) voting available or not. Most states have none; a handful do.
- Ranked-choice voting (e.g., Maine, Alaska, and cities such as NYC primaries and parts of Utah and California): explain ranking, and that you may rank only one.
- Undervoting: skipping a race is allowed and the rest of the ballot still counts.
- Phones and electronic devices: some states ban their use in the booth or within a set distance. State the rule.
- Printed notes or sample ballots in the booth: most states allow them. State the rule.
- Assistance: voters may bring someone to help (not an employer or union rep) or ask poll workers. Accessible voting machines are available.
- Electioneering: no campaign shirts, buttons or hats within the buffer zone in many states. Give the distance.
- Spoiled ballot: you can ask for a replacement, usually up to a fixed number of times.

**After voting**
- How to track a mail or provisional ballot, if the state offers tracking.

## Writing the box

- **First-time-voter version.** 6–10 short bullets covering the items that matter for **in-person** voting in this state. Add mail-voting bullets where relevant. Plain words, no jargon.
- **Short version** (when the audience is not a first-time voter). 3–4 bullets: ID, the remaining early-voting window, Election Day hours, and the phone/notes rule.
- **Sourcing.** Cite the state or county office page for each fact. If a fact can't be confirmed, write "Check with [office name]" and link it.
