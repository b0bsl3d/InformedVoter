Keller Ballot Guide 2026

Every contest on your ballot, in exact ballot order. Numbers 1–71 match the ballot and the one-page cheat sheet. Contested races get a full box; uncontested races are a single line in their ballot position. This guide is nonpartisan and recommends no candidate or bond vote.

First time voting? Bring a Texas photo ID (driver's license, state ID, passport or similar). Texas has no straight-party option, so you mark each race separately, and you may skip any race. You can bring printed notes such as this guide, but you can't use your phone at the voting station.

Federal & Statewide Offices #1–9

U.S. Senate and U.S. House, then Texas's statewide executive offices. In the UH Hobby “Texas Votes 2026” poll (Oct. 10–14), the closest statewide races are U.S. Senate and Railroad Commissioner (2 points each) and Governor (3 points). In the other statewide races below, the Republican leads by 5–7 points; most leads are near the margin of error. No Democrat has won statewide in Texas since 1994.

§

The 2025 split: background and where candidates stand

Context for the three trustee races below

  • 2024: A plan to split the district along U.S. 377 is discussed in closed session with a consultant (a lawsuit cites an Oct. 10, 2024 session and says the idea dates to 2022). Two trustees say they first learned of it Dec. 19. Board president Charles Randklev cited about $45M in budget cuts and a goal of keeping tax dollars local; supporters also cited falling enrollment and thin state funding.
  • The math: A MoakCasey study projected about 21,700 students on the west (mostly Fort Worth) side and 9,100 on the east (Keller) side. Per-student funding would be roughly $10,200 west and $9,200 east, largely because the west would draw more state aid for special education, low-income and bilingual students. A consultant's email warned costs for underfunded programs would shift to the new western district.
  • Jan.–Mar. 2025: Heavy backlash, student walkouts and the superintendent's resignation. A Fort Worth resident sued, later joined by the Heritage HOA, which petitioned to remove five trustees it said backed the plan: Randklev, John Birt, Micah Young, Chris Coker and Heather Washington. The plan was dropped March 14.
  • Feb. 2025: During the split debate, Fort Worth council members moved to rezone 23 KISD properties to community-facility use only. None of the candidates' public statements found address Fort Worth annexation of the western area.
  • May 2025: The board voted unanimously not to pursue detachment. A bill requiring a public vote on any split (HB 5089) stalled in the Texas House and did not pass.

District's case

  • First bond since 2019 ($315M, which the district says finished on budget).
  • Citizens Bond Advisory Committee approved each item by two-thirds; trustees approved 5–1.
  • The district projects all five can be paid for at the existing 33¢ debt rate, which it says would stay the same whether or not they pass.
  • District estimates delay could cost about $50M as construction costs rise 6%+ a year; bond money isn't subject to recapture.

Opposition and caveats

  • True Texas Project urges “no.” Coker, Parrish (A and B) and Hodges question size or structure.
  • No formal pro-bond PAC found. Weatherington, Walsh, Brewer and Johnson support it.
  • “No rate increase” is a projection that depends on property values and interest rates. The district's fiscal page says the rate would stay the same whether or not the propositions pass. No per-home estimate was published.
